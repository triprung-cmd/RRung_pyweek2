# Primal Mango Ice Cream

Custard-base mango ice cream built for raw cream, raw milk, egg yolks, and a ripe mango. Sweetened with honey. No refined sugar, no seed oils, no weird fillers.

Makes about 1 quart · prep 25 min · chill 4+ hrs · churn ~20–30 min

---

## Ingredients

| Amount | Ingredient |
|--------|------------|
| 1 | ripe mango (Ataulfo/honey mango if you can get it — sweeter, less fibrous) |
| 1½ cups (360 ml) | raw cream |
| ¾ cup (180 ml) | raw milk |
| 4 | egg yolks (from pastured eggs if possible) |
| ⅓–½ cup (110–170 g) | raw honey (start with ⅓ cup; taste after blending — mangoes vary) |
| 1 tsp | vanilla extract (or ½ scraped vanilla bean) |
| ⅛ tsp | fine sea salt |
| 1 tsp | fresh lime zest (optional but highly recommended) |
| ½–1 tsp | fresh lime juice (optional — brightens the mango) |

**Optional upgrades**
- Pinch of ground cardamom or grated fresh ginger with the custard for warmth
- 1 tsp gelatin bloomed in 2 Tbsp cold milk, melted into the warm base — softer scoop after a hard freeze

---

## Method

### 1. Mango puree
Peel and pit the mango. Blend the flesh until completely smooth. You want about **¾–1 cup puree**. Strain through a fine mesh if it’s fibrous. Set aside.

### 2. Custard base
1. Whisk the egg yolks, honey, salt, and vanilla in a medium bowl until the mixture looks pale and slightly thick.
2. Warm the milk and **½ cup of the cream** in a small saucepan over medium-low heat until steaming (small bubbles at the edge — do not boil).
3. Temper: drizzle the hot dairy into the yolks while whisking constantly so the eggs don’t scramble.
4. Pour everything back into the pan. Cook over low heat, stirring constantly with a spatula, until the custard coats the back of a spoon (~170–175°F / 77–80°C). Do not boil.
5. Immediately strain into a clean bowl.

### 3. Finish the base
Whisk in the remaining **1 cup cold cream**, the mango puree, lime zest, and lime juice. Taste. It should taste a touch sweeter and brighter than you want the frozen ice cream to be — freezing mutes both.

### 4. Chill hard
Cover and refrigerate until completely cold, **at least 4 hours**, overnight preferred. Cold base = creamier churn.

### 5. Churn
Churn in an ice cream maker per the manufacturer’s instructions until soft-serve thick. Transfer to a freezer container, press parchment on the surface, and freeze **2–4 hours** until scoopable.

---

## No ice cream maker?

1. Pour the chilled base into a shallow metal pan.
2. Freeze 45 minutes, then whisk hard to break up ice crystals.
3. Repeat every 30–45 minutes for 3–4 rounds until thick and creamy.
4. Or freeze in ice cube trays, then blend cubes in a high-speed blender until fluffy and re-freeze briefly.

---

## Keep-it-rawer variation

If you want to avoid heating the dairy:

1. Blend mango, yolks, honey, vanilla, salt, and lime until silky.
2. Whisk in cold raw cream + raw milk.
3. Chill, then churn as above.

Use the freshest pastured yolks you trust; this skip leaves the eggs uncooked.

---

## Why it hits

- **Yolks** = richness and that slow-melt custard mouthfeel
- **High cream-to-milk** = less ice, more scoop
- **Honey** = primal sweetener with slight floral depth that loves mango
- **Lime + salt** = keeps it from tasting flat or candy-sweet
- **Cold base + hard chill after churn** = the difference between icy and hella good

---

## Storage

Best within a week. Soften on the counter 5–10 minutes before scooping. If it freezes rock-hard, next time add the gelatin option or an extra tablespoon of honey.
