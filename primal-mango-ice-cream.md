# Primal Mango Ice Cream (No Machine)

From start to scoopable. No ice cream maker. Honey-sweetened custard base with raw cream, raw milk, egg yolks, and mango.

Makes about 1 quart.

---

## What you need

**Ingredients**
- 1 ripe mango
- 1½ cups raw cream
- ¾ cup raw milk
- 4 egg yolks
- ⅓–½ cup raw honey
- 1 tsp vanilla extract
- ⅛ tsp fine sea salt
- 1 tsp lime zest (optional, but worth it)
- ½–1 tsp lime juice (optional)

**Gear**
- Blender or food processor (or a fork + fine mesh strainer)
- Medium bowl
- Whisk
- Small saucepan
- Spatula
- Fine mesh strainer
- Shallow metal pan (baking pan / loaf tin — metal freezes faster)
- Freezer space

---

## Step-by-step

### Step 1 — Prep the mango
1. Peel the mango.
2. Cut the flesh off the pit.
3. Blend until completely smooth (or mash hard with a fork, then push through a strainer).
4. You want about **¾–1 cup puree**. Set aside.

### Step 2 — Set up the yolks
1. Separate 4 eggs. Keep the yolks; stash the whites for something else.
2. In a medium bowl, whisk together:
   - the 4 yolks
   - **⅓ cup honey** (you can add more later)
   - **⅛ tsp salt**
   - **1 tsp vanilla**
3. Whisk until it looks a little pale and thickened. Set aside.

### Step 3 — Warm the dairy
1. Put **¾ cup raw milk** and **½ cup raw cream** in the saucepan.
2. Heat on medium-low until steaming — little bubbles at the edges.
3. Do **not** boil. Turn off / pull off heat when it’s hot and steaming.

### Step 4 — Temper the eggs (don’t scramble them)
1. While whisking the yolk mixture constantly, slowly drizzle in the hot milk/cream, a little at a time.
2. Once about half the hot dairy is in, pour the rest in while still whisking.
3. Pour everything back into the saucepan.

### Step 5 — Cook the custard
1. Put the pan on **low** heat.
2. Stir constantly with a spatula, scraping the bottom and corners.
3. Cook until the custard thickens enough to **coat the back of a spoon** (you can draw a clean line with your finger through the coating). Aim for about **170–175°F** if you have a thermometer.
4. Pull it off heat as soon as it’s there. Do **not** boil or you’ll get sweet scrambled eggs.

### Step 6 — Strain and finish the base
1. Pour the hot custard through a fine mesh strainer into a clean bowl.
2. Whisk in:
   - the remaining **1 cup cold raw cream**
   - the mango puree
   - lime zest and lime juice (if using)
3. Taste it. It should taste a bit **sweeter and brighter** than you want the ice cream — freezing dulls both.
4. Want it sweeter? Whisk in more honey now, up to ½ cup total.

### Step 7 — Chill until cold
1. Cover the bowl.
2. Refrigerate until the base is fully cold — **at least 4 hours**, overnight is better.
3. Cold base = creamier ice cream. Don’t skip this.

### Step 8 — Freeze and whip (this is your “machine”)
1. Pour the cold base into a **shallow metal pan**.
2. Put it in the freezer, uncovered, for **45 minutes**.
3. Pull it out. The edges will be firming up; the center still soft.
4. Whisk hard (or scrape and mash with a fork) until smooth again — break up every icy bit.
5. Return to the freezer.
6. Repeat every **30–45 minutes**, whisking hard each time, for about **3–4 rounds** total (roughly 2–3 hours).
7. When it’s thick like soft-serve and mostly frozen, you’re done whisking.

### Step 9 — Final freeze
1. Smooth the top.
2. Cover with parchment pressed onto the surface (stops ice crystals), then wrap or lid the pan.
3. Freeze **2–4 more hours** until scoopable.
4. Soften on the counter **5–10 minutes** before scooping if it’s rock-hard.

## If it freezes too hard next time

- Add an extra tablespoon of honey to the base, or
- Bloom 1 tsp gelatin in 2 Tbsp cold milk, melt into the warm custard before straining

---

## Quick troubleshooting

| Problem | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Custard scrambled | Heat was too high. Strain hard and keep going — texture may be a little grainy. |
| Icy / crunchy | More aggressive whisking next time, or a shallower pan. |
| Too sweet / not sweet | Adjust honey before freezing; frozen = less sweet. |
| Bland mango | Use a riper mango, or add the lime. |
